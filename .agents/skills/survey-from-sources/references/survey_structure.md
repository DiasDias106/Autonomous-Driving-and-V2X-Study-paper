# Survey structure

Use this template. Adapt section names to the language of the text, and merge or drop sections when the material is small, but keep the order: it is the order readers of a survey expect.

## 1. Title and abstract
- Title: names the main topic and the angle ("A survey of X", "X: estado da arte").
- Abstract (120–200 words): scope, what is covered, how the sources were organised, main takeaways, open problems.

## 2. Introduction
- What the main topic is and why it matters (2–3 sentences).
- Where it sits: its past and its neighbouring topics, in a short paragraph (details come later in section 3).
- Scope: what the survey covers and what it deliberately leaves out.
- Organisation: one sentence announcing each following section.

## 3. Background and context
- **Historical antecedents**: how the topic emerged, in chronological order and brief.
- **Neighbouring topics**: one short subsection per neighbour, each stating its relationship to the main topic and ending with a bridge back to it (see `bridging.md`).
- **Key definitions and notation** needed later.

## 4. Core of the survey: taxonomy and review
- Present a taxonomy: the categories into which the work on the topic falls (by approach, problem, period, method, application...). Say why this division was chosen.
- One subsection per category: idea, representative works from the sources, strengths and limitations.
- Where useful, one comparison table across categories (criteria in columns, approaches in rows).
- This section carries most of the word count.

## 5. Discussion
- Cross-cutting patterns, agreements and disagreements between sources.
- Trade-offs and conditions under which each approach works.

## 6. Open problems and future directions
- Gaps the sources reveal; questions that remain unresolved; plausible next steps. Label speculation as such.

## 7. Conclusion
- Three to five sentences restating the main topic, the main findings of the survey and its position relative to neighbouring topics. No new information.

## 8. References
- Only sources actually cited, in a consistent format. If metadata is missing, use the file name and say so.

## Clarity checklist
- Topic sentence first in every paragraph.
- Headings are informative ("Approaches based on X"), not vague ("Other things").
- Max three heading levels.
- No paragraph over about 150 words.
- Same term for the same concept everywhere.
