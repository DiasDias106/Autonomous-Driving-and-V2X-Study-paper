# Review Checklist

Use as a reference checklist per dimension. It is not necessary to comment on every item: only address those that apply and provide actionable insight.

## 1. Structure
- Does the title accurately describe the scope of the document?
- Is there an abstract, and does it genuinely reflect the content and conclusions?
- Does the introduction provide context, problem statement, objectives, contributions, and a document roadmap?
- Does the sequence of sections follow a logical order (chronological, thematic, general to specific)?
- Are sections balanced in depth and length (e.g., avoiding an 8-page section next to an unjustified 2-paragraph section)?
- Are there single-subsection sections (an indicator of poor hierarchical planning)?
- Is there duplicated content across different sections, or content placed in the wrong section?
- Is the heading hierarchy consistent and numbering correct?
- Does the conclusion synthesise key findings, extract implications, and suggest future work without introducing unvetted new material?
- Are mandatory sections missing (limitations, methodology, glossary, future work)?

## 2. Vocabulary and Language
- Consistent terminology: is the same concept referred to with a single consistent term? (e.g., avoiding erratic switching between "framework", "architecture", and "platform")
- Acronyms expanded on first use and used consistently thereafter.
- Technical terms defined before being applied.
- Tone and register appropriate for the document type (academic, formal, technical).
- Avoidance of vague filler words ("several", "very", "a lot", "etc.") where precision is required.
- Unsupported absolute claims ("always", "never", "all").
- Sentences exceeding ~40 words or concatenating too many disparate clauses.
- Excessive passive voice, heavy nominalisations, or repetitive vocabulary in close proximity.
- Unintentional language mixing or regional dialect inconsistencies (e.g., US vs. UK English).
- Grammatical, spelling, and punctuation errors.

## 3. Intertextual Cohesion
- Does every significant factual claim have a citation or empirical evidence?
- Is every reference in the bibliography cited in the text, and does every in-text citation have a matching bibliography entry?
- Are sources critically discussed and compared, or merely enumerated ("X did A. Y did B. Z did C.")?
- Is there clear positioning relative to the literature (what is agreed with, what remains missing, where the research gap lies)?
- Are sources recent and reputable? Is there over-reliance on too few sources?
- Are there contradictions between sections or between body prose and cited literature?
- Is the citation style applied consistently?
- Figures and tables: numbered, captioned, referenced in the text, and critically interpreted (not just "see Figure 2").

## 4. Connections Between Sections
- Does each section open with its purpose and close with a bridge to the following section?
- Is there a coherent narrative thread ("golden thread"): can the document's argument be followed purely by reading the transitions?
- Do cross-references ("in Section IV") point to the correct sections and figures?
- Are promises made in the introduction delivered in subsequent sections? Do conclusions strictly follow from the body?
- Are concepts introduced in earlier sections reused naturally where relevant, rather than re-introduced as if new?
- Are there abrupt narrative jumps (e.g., a section assuming context only explained later)?

## 5. Content
- Depth appropriate for the intended target audience.
- Topical coverage: what was omitted, and is that omission justified or explicitly scoped out?
- Examples, use cases, or data supporting primary claims.
- Technical rigour: terms used correctly, numerical metrics consistent.
- Quality of argumentation: premises, evidence, deduction.
- Explicit acknowledgement of limitations.

## 6. Extras for Surveys
- Clear scope and explicitly stated research questions.
- Literature selection criteria (databases, search period, keywords, inclusion/exclusion rules).
- Clear taxonomy or classification scheme applied consistently.
- Systematic comparison across works (comparative tables, explicit evaluation criteria).
- Identification of research gaps and open challenges.
- Future research directions tied directly to identified gaps.
- Novel contribution/positioning relative to existing surveys on the same topic.
